I really liked this book. The criteria it gives you for thinking about management feel spot on.

Overall, it helps answer two questions: how do you know if management suits you, and how do you know if you're being a bad, good, or great manager?

It's very practical, with examples I found relatable. Julie used to work at Facebook, now Meta, so the examples hit home.

## Is management right for you?

Imagine a day in your life as a manager:

1. If you had back-to-back meetings all day, would you be happy or drained?
2. If you had to give someone feedback, would you enjoy it or avoid it if you could?
3. If you needed to convince someone to do X, Y, or Z, would the process of persuading them feel satisfying?

If you enjoy all three, I see that as a sign that management could suit you. But you still need to learn what separates bad, good, and great management.

## How do you know you're a good manager?

A few indicators I found useful:

- Do your direct reports consistently bring their biggest challenges to you?
- Can they give and receive feedback without taking it personally?
- Would they choose to have you as their manager again?

## What makes a manager great?

At a minimum, you need to think about your multiplier effect: how you can make the whole team more effective. You also need to care about your direct reports and really listen to them.

But what makes a manager great is the team's actual results. Having a friendly team where everything runs smoothly isn't enough if the team doesn't make an impact.

You need to balance both: care for your people while making sure the team delivers strong results against the metrics you're responsible for.
